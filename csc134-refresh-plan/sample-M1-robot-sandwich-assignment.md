# Assignment: The Robot Sandwich

*Module One — Talk to Computers (and Your Team)*

---

## The situation

You have a robot. It is strong, fast, perfectly obedient — and it has **zero common sense.**

It will do exactly what you say. Not what you meant. What you *said.*

Your job: write instructions that get this robot to make a sandwich, start to finish.

## Why we're doing this

This is what programming is. A computer is the robot: powerful, obedient, and completely literal. Every bug you will ever write comes from the gap between what you meant and what you said. This assignment is your first rep at closing that gap — before there's any code to hide behind.

It's also your first practice at something you'll do all semester: writing a clear document in Markdown and submitting it correctly through GitHub. In this field, work that isn't submitted correctly might as well not exist. Harsh, but true — and easy to get right once you've done it.

## What to write

Create a Markdown file containing your instructions. It must include:

1. **A title** (a top-level heading).
2. **An "Assumptions" section.** What's the starting state? Is the bread already out? Is the robot's hand empty? Where is everything? You decide — but you have to say so. Unstated assumptions are where instructions fail.
3. **Numbered steps.** Each step is one action. Small, specific, in order.
4. **A "Done When" line.** How does the robot know the job is finished?

Remember: the robot doesn't know what "open the bread" means. Is the bread in a bag? How does a bag open? Be the person who thought of that.

| Format | 4 | Real Markdown: headings render, list numbers correctly |
| Submission | 4 | Right file name, right folder, right repo, committed |

No hidden criteria. What's on this page is the whole rubric.

## How to know you're done

## Fair warning

In class, we may act some of these out — one of us plays the robot, and the robot is *not generous.* If your step 4 says "put the peanut butter on the bread," expect to watch a sealed jar get set on top of a bag of bread. This is the fun kind of failure. Take notes; revise; resubmit before the due date if you want.

That loop — write, test, watch it fail, fix it — is the whole job. You're already doing it.
