## What you're actually here to learn

This course teaches you Python. That's true, but it's not the point.

The point is this: **you're here to learn how to solve problems with systems.** Python is the tool we'll use to practice. By the end of the semester, you'll take an idea, turn it into a plan, turn the plan into working code, test it, fix it, and show it to other people. That whole path — idea to shipped — is the job. Everything in this course is a piece of it.

## What is Information Technology?

IT is the field of building and running the systems that people and organizations depend on. That covers a lot of ground: the network in this building, the database behind your bank account, the app you used this morning, and the person who gets called when any of those break.

Some of you are headed toward programming. Some of you are headed toward networks, security, or support. This course is the shared front door. The skills here — precise thinking, precise writing, and knowing how software actually gets made — belong to every one of those paths.

## What is a system?

Here's the definition we'll use all semester, so get comfortable with it:

> **A system is people, processes, and technology — working together.**

Not just technology. Here's why that matters.

Think about the self-checkout machines at a supermarket. Impressive technology: scanners, scales, payment processing, theft detection. Now remove the people. Nobody stocks the shelves. Nobody fixes the machine when it jams. Nobody defined the process for what happens when your coupon won't scan.

What do you have? A kiosk in an empty room. The technology only means something inside the system.

When you build software, you are never just building software. You're building one part of a system that includes the people who use it and the processes they follow. The best people in this field never forget that. Now you won't either — you learned it in week one.

## The question nobody asks out loud

Let's just say it: **"Why should I learn this when AI can write code?"**

It's a fair question. It deserves a straight answer, so here it is.

If you're not going into IT — honestly, we won't argue with you. Use the tools, live your life.

But if you **are** going into IT, follow the logic. When an AI writes code for you, someone has to answer one question before that code goes anywhere: *did the AI even understand the problem?* Answering that question requires you to understand the problem — the system, the people, the process, the code. There is no shortcut around that. There's no version of this career where you get to skip knowing what you're doing.

So here's the honest version: **even if your plan is to have AI do most of the work, that plan only works if you're good enough to check the work.** That's what this course builds. And "I prompt AI and hope for the best" is not a skill set anyone is hiring for.

We're not anti-AI here. Later in this course, you'll use AI as a real collaborator — and by then, you'll be qualified to supervise it.

## How this course works

A few promises, so you know what you're walking into:

- **Every module ends with a checkpoint quiz.** It's low-stakes. It's there to make sure you're ready for what's next, because each module builds on the last. You complete it, you move on.
- **There are two tests: a midterm and a final. There are no trick questions.** We mean that. If you did the work, the tests will feel familiar. Tests here measure whether you learned the material — not whether you can survive an ambush.
- **You will fail sometimes, on purpose.** Programs break. Yours will too. In this course, an error message isn't a judgment — it's information, and reading it is a skill we will practice together. Failure is exercise. Put in the reps.
